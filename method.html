Method

How peak areas become concentrations, and which checks are applied on the way. The same engine runs in the browser and in the Node test suite (npm test).

1 · Calibration

For each standard the response is the quantifier-ion area of the analyte divided by that of the internal standard (IS). Without an IS (external calibration) the raw area is used.

y = Aanalyte / AIS    fitted as    y = m·x + b

x is the nominal concentration in the vial (µM). Fits are weighted by 1/x² by default, because the standards span 50-fold and the absolute error grows with concentration; an unweighted fit is dominated by the top standard and biases low-level samples. 1/x, unweighted and through-origin fits are available for comparison.

Every standard is back-calculated, x̂ = (y − b)/m. Standards whose x̂ deviates from nominal by more than 15 % (20 % at the lowest level) are removed one at a time, worst first, and the curve is refitted. Removal stops at five concentration levels; inner standards are only removed while at least 75 % of the standards remain. The accepted lowest and highest levels define LLOQ and ULOQ.

LOD and LOQ are also estimated from the curve (ICH Q2): σ is the residual standard deviation of an unweighted fit over the lowest three levels, LOD = 3.3σ/m and LOQ = 10σ/m.

An analyte is treated as absent from a set of standards when its response does not rise with concentration (Spearman ρ ≤ 0.8, or a signal increase of less than 20 % of the concentration span). This stops a curve being fitted to background noise.

The qualifier/quantifier area ratio (transition 2 / transition 1) is averaged over the accepted standards.

2 · Samples

Cvial = (y − b) / m × (CIS, sample / CIS, standards)
Creaction = Cvial × dilution factor

If samples and standards received the same IS concentration the middle factor is 1, and differences in absolute IS response between the two runs (e.g. instrument sensitivity) cancel out. Negative back-calculated values are reported as 0.

When several calibrations are listed for an experiment, each analyte uses the first one in which it has an accepted curve. The IS used for a sample is always the IS of that curve.

Flags

  • IS missing — IS area below 10 % of the run median: no value is reported.
  • IS deviation — IS area outside 50–150 % of the run median.
  • not detected / ≤ 3× blank — no peak, or a peak not above three times the median solvent blank.
  • < LLOQ / > ULOQ — outside the accepted calibration range (ULOQ with a 15 % margin). Values are kept but are estimates; above-range samples should be diluted and re-injected.
  • ion ratio — qualifier/quantifier ratio more than 30 % from the standards.

Flags never remove data by themselves. Exclusions are explicit: in the experiment registry (with a reason) or by unticking a row in the data table.

3 · Relative mode

When the samples contain no IS matching any calibration, results are reported as the response (area ratio to a surrogate IS, or raw area) relative to the mean t₀ response of the same condition and substrate. No µM values are produced.

4 · Statistics

  • Replicates are summarised as mean ± SD (n − 1).
  • % remaining is computed per replicate against the mean t₀ value, then averaged.
  • End-point designs: Welch's unequal-variance t-test between the first and last time point.
  • Time courses: first-order decay C = C₀·e−kt, fitted by linear regression of ln C on t over all replicates that are detected and not below LLOQ. The 95 % CI of k uses the t distribution; t½ = ln 2 / k.

5 · Figures

Every chart has SVG and PNG buttons that re-render it in print style (white background, Arial, black axes) at a journal column width: 89 mm single column or 183 mm double column, PNG at 600 dpi.
